# Ten

Ten is current **Staff**, but the archive's best Ten material does not come from explaining Staff at all. It comes from watching how little runway Ten needs before entering somebody else's premise. Animal noise, screenshot-jail inheritance law, fake violence, family-tree bureaucracy, bot-command affection — whatever dialect the room has made usable, Ten tends to answer inside it rather than standing outside and explaining the joke.

That pattern is present from the front door. On August 14, 2020, Ten's recovered Lobby entrance is simply **`*barks*`**. Gilli answers **`HI BB`**, handles the actual onboarding, and says `Role assigned!`; the line never names the role, so it cannot be turned into a dated Staff appointment. Mugen arrives with **`Woof`**. Anayss eventually supplies the conventional **`Welcome!!`**. Ten never gives the archive a polished introduction. The room already understands the stage direction.

The same compact mirroring keeps appearing in smaller Club Only fragments: Gilli throws `owO`; Ten answers `owo;`. Gilli says morning; Ten gives `mornin`. These are supporting texture rather than separate biography chapters, but they show why the entrance is not a one-off gimmick. Ten often meets a direct bid in the register it arrived in.

September 10 gives the Wall version. Ten writes: **`i'm leaving a legacy here too apparently. i only hope my heir lives up to this.`** Mugen and Anthos both react `Hehe`. The useful thing is not the embarrassment itself but Ten's choice of scale. Screenshot jail is apparently no longer a post; it is an estate. There is an heir. The room laughs with the frame immediately. None of this establishes governance succession. It establishes that Ten understands being archived as something ridiculous enough to require inheritance planning.

Two weeks later the available dialect is mock danger. Ten sees a snitch premise and answers **`a snitch u say`**, then seven seconds later **`*gets hammer*`**. In a nearby escalation Gilli jokes about destroying Ansun's body; Ten contributes **`if they die they die`**. Illien laughs almost immediately. Ten closes with **`owo`**. The sequence is useful precisely because the `owo` punctures any attempt to read the hammer literally. This is premise uptake, not an enforcement job.

Then Anthos asks Ten to do bureaucracy. On September 29, she asks what role Ten wants in the Tumbleweed. Ten proposes **`anthos apprentice like in game`**, explains **`cos head empty rn`**, offers **`or whatever rooks said`**, and finally reports **`i'm just kinda feeling the anomaly vibe`**. Rooks solves the administrative crisis by offering to adopt Ten. Ten accepts: **`that's fine tbh lol`**.

This is exactly the kind of scene the new wiki needs to keep in full rather than compress into a badge called `Apprentice`. The archive is not documenting an appointment. It is documenting a family-tree form losing a fight with vibes. Anthos asks for taxonomy; Ten submits an existing game relationship, openly announces that the naming department is offline, borrows Rooks's answer, then gets adopted as a workaround.

October 1 changes the register again. Mugen sends **`eli kiss @Ten!`**. Two minutes later Ten returns **`eli kiss @Mugen`**. Later in the burst Ten posts **`cuddle puddle`**, though the reply metadata does not prove that line is specifically about Mugen. The exact reciprocal syntax is the character beat. Mock violence gets mock violence; affection gets affection. The same willingness to inhabit the room's current language works in both directions.

There are unresolved Ten reaction hits elsewhere — attachment-only Wall posts, emoji-letter piles, shared reactions with Ren — but they stay backstage when the target object is unclear. The five clean beats already make a better character file than a generic `chaotic Staff member` label could. Ten barks and gets barked at. He discusses Wall embarrassment as inheritable property. A snitch appears and he has a hammer in seven seconds. Asked for a Tumbleweed role, he submits `head empty` and accepts adoption. Mugen sends affection in bot syntax; Ten returns the exact command.

The through-line is not chaos for its own sake. It is **low-friction participation**: Ten rarely needs the room to translate itself before he joins in.
