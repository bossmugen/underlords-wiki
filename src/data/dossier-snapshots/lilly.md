# Lilly

Lilly is current **Staff**, but her archive file is at its best when nobody is explaining a role. She enters surviving scenes through collision: somebody welcomes her, somebody shouts her name, somebody proposes jail, somebody else starts pouting, and Lilly answers in whatever register the room has already chosen. The result is less a résumé than a sequence of increasingly UL ways to say **yes, I am here with you**.

The first clean receipt arrives at the Lobby on July 1, 2020. Lilly introduces herself as **`ButteryButtz (aka lilly), Aether, secretary`**. Snow answers: **`Welcome Lilly with two L's please enjoy your stay at the strip club . Role assigned`**. The exchange does several useful things at once. It gives Lilly a self-attested alias. It gives Snow a direct intake scene. It also gives the wiki a reason not to hallucinate: `Aether, secretary` belongs to Lilly's period self-description and is not a dated UL Secretary appointment. Snow's `Lilly with two L's` is a joke about spelling, not a new alias requiring its own passport.

By October the front-door formality has evaporated. In the Wall, Lilly's first surfaced business is telling Danny: **`stop simping over the girls here`**. Ansun then welcomes her to **`our humble abode`**, and Lilly answers with a polite little `heheheheh thank you`. The timing is the character beat: she is already scolding somebody before the welcome has finished landing. Nothing in the scene makes her moderator of Simping Affairs. It is simply ordinary peer banter arriving before the ceremonial hello.

The same night the room gets louder. Ren posts **`LILLYYYY`**. Ansun contributes **`MAAM`**. Lilly answers Ren with **`I LOVE YOU`**. The archive supports mutual recognition and affection in the moment; it does not need a romance plot or comparative best-friend ranking grafted onto three shouted lines. What matters is how quickly Lilly shifts from pointing at Danny to being loudly recognized herself. She is easiest to see when other people hit the scene at the same time.

December turns that relational reflex into one of the simplest jokes in the file. Shiki says **`so i can go jail chat`**. Lilly replies **`ill join you in jail bb`**. Shiki says **`jail ok`**. There is no recovered disciplinary hearing, no proof of sanctions, no need to solve what crime has allegedly occurred. The human part is smaller and funnier: Shiki names jail; Lilly's first response is company.

Then the reaction system gives Lilly and Rooks an entire dialogue without conventional sentences. On December 2, one Wall exhibit accumulates at least **nineteen separate named pout variants** whose two resolved reactors are exactly Rooks and Lilly: `poutmad`, `alicepout`, `GengarPout`, `NekoPout`, `rin_pout`, `Razor_Pout`, and on and on until the reaction tray itself has become a bit. A separate pout reaction includes Illien too and is kept separate rather than quietly rounded into the duet. Nineteen is already stupid enough; we do not need to cheat.

That pile is useful because it makes a recurring UL behavior visible at person scale. Reactions are not decoration sitting under the real conversation. Sometimes the reaction vocabulary *is* the conversation. Rooks and Lilly repeatedly choosing matching pout variants on the same exhibit creates a synchronized performance without proving that they were uniquely close, privately coordinating, or responsible for the image they were reacting to.

Later Lilly attachment posts produce direct mentions and even a true Illien reply, but the underlying images were not visually inspected in the source pass. Those receipts stay downstairs rather than letting an unseen screenshot dictate a sixth scene. The current five beats already form a clean progression: arrive through a ridiculous welcome, start bantering before the welcome finishes, get loudly recognized, volunteer for jail company, then become half of the Wall's Pout Department.

That is enough character. Lilly's file does not need the wiki to invent a philosophy around it. The archive already shows the thing worth keeping: she repeatedly becomes legible through other people, and she answers fast enough that the encounter itself becomes the story.
