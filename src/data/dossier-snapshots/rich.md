# Rich

Amaurot's club leader.

## S10 OPENING-ERA POWER MAP

Mugs-confirmed context: when Dragon Raja first opened on S10, **Amaurot was one of the top three clubs**.

**ScarletMoon was #1.**

That matters because UL's VIP list was not only casual visitors. Some visitors came from major neighboring clubs with leadership structures and reputations of their own.

## BEFORE THE CORPSE ROOM, HE WAS ALREADY CONNECTING ROOMS

The 2020 archive finally makes Rich's leadership useful at character scale instead of leaving it as a title in the Case File.

On April 7, Snow asks him in the UL Lobby:

> **hey rich anyone else want to join from Amaurot?**

The tiny word doing all the work is **else**. Snow is not introducing two strangers or asking the room at large. She goes to Rich as the obvious Amaurot-side contact and asks whether more people want through the door. The archive does not let us assign individual recruits to him from that sentence, but it does show what the relationship between clubs looked like in motion: Rich was somebody UL could ask when it needed to reach Amaurot people.

Five days later, Mugen announces to UL that **“DragonRich (Amaurot Leader) has created an S10 channel for anyone interested”** and drops the invite. Different scene, same instinct. Rich is not only the head of one club guarding one roster; he is building a room above the club boundary and UL is advertising it to its own people.

This is delicious in hindsight because Amaurot later merged under UL. The April receipts do **not** prove that these invitations caused the merger or tell us when the merger happened. They show something more human and more useful: before the institutions converged, the relationship was already porous. People had doors into one another's spaces. Rich was standing near one of those doors.

## UL FILE

Rich belongs under VIP because that category preserves his external-club lineage. His Amaurot leadership is not retroactively a UL command rank, and the later merger does not turn `Amaurot Leader` into `UL Officer` by paperwork magic.

## INTERCLUB CONTEXT

Zyrcant was Rich's deputy in Amaurot before the merger. Woosung, the former ScarletMoon leader, supplies the other side of the S10 powerhouse context.

Taken together, these are not random guest cameos. UL's social world was already overlapping with other organized groups, complete with their own leaders, deputies, and migration paths.

## 2024 — RICHARD'S CORPSE BECOMES A LIVING ROOM

The late voice-room archive gives Rich a completely different kind of relevance.

On October 28, 2024, Life/Sye's surviving text reacts for roughly two hours while Rich appears to be cooking and talking: fire-safety warnings, garlic, dinner, work, MMOs, PC parts, geography, donuts. Rich himself contributes almost no text.

That asymmetry is the point. In voice, a person can be socially central while leaving almost no keyboard trace.

Two days later Momo says she had fallen asleep and that Rich's voice startled her awake during an apparent shared-watch/stream session. By December, Tae is watching Rich play another game, considering downloading it, wondering whether Rummy would like it, and saying they will join another day if Rich is streaming.

This gives Rich an unexpectedly tidy long arc. In 2020 he is helping connect people **between clubs**. By 2024 people are gathering in a room with his name on it simply because he is there cooking, talking, or showing them a game. The scale collapses from S10 infrastructure to hanging out in somebody's digital kitchen, but the underlying move barely changes: **make a room, let people cross into it.**

That is much more interesting than `former neighboring-club boss`. The leadership title survives as history; the behavior survives as hospitality.

## THE DATA TRAP

If we ranked late VC by text messages alone, Rich would look strangely absent from his own room.

The surrounding messages prove why that would be nonsense. Other people repeatedly address him, react to what he is apparently saying or streaming, and organize their own attention around him.

So Rich's file carries a methodological warning too: **voice-channel text volume is not voice participation.**
