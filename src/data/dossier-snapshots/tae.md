Tae does not wait until she has the polished version of anything. She asks the algebra question while she's still figuring out how to phrase it, announces a new rule against Gabu without bothering to convene a government, and contributes evidence to the Wall even though the Wall keeps putting her on trial. Ren once announced she'd become one of them. Tae answered `I been in here`—a correction of tense, not affection. She already knew she belonged.

Anthos will explain the brackets. Mugs will turn mathematics into a bedtime weapon. Gabu will be sent to jail under a law Tae invented that morning. And years later, when Rich is playing something else, Tae is already wondering whether Rummy might want to join. The game changes. The habit of dragging friends into the next ridiculous thing does not.

## Ask Anthos, Not Google

On August 27, 2020, the surviving Lobby scene gives Tae exactly one word: `Underling`. A few minutes later Mugs answers with `Role assigned, welcome` and sends her toward the Discord Tour. That is a clean Discord doorway. It is not a magic first-membership timestamp; whatever in-game history produced `Underling` is not safely reconstructed from these two lines.

A month later the room already feels less like an intake form. Tae opens Club Only with `Stupid question but`, then asks `Whats uhh 5(2a+7)` and immediately clarifies: `But simplified🧍🏽‍♀️`.

The wording is tiny but revealing. `Stupid question but` tries to lower the cost of asking before the question has even arrived. `Whats uhh` keeps the hesitation visible. Then `But simplified` snaps into specificity: Tae may be embarrassed to ask, but she knows exactly what she needs from the room.

Anthos eventually answers `10a + 35`, then comes back with a full BODMAS walkthrough. The archive does not say homework, class, exam, or whose equation this originally was. It says something better for a relationship file: Tae throws an ordinary math problem into the club room and Anthos actually sits down to explain it.

That makes later Mugs-versus-Tae advanced-math nonsense funnier. Before mathematics becomes a sleep-deprivation comedy device, Club Only has already been used for the much more reasonable service of helping Tae expand brackets.

## Gabu Is Under Arrest

On January 3 Tae announces `new rule`. Five seconds later the statute arrives in full: `Always vote out gabu`.

The speed is excellent. There is no committee hearing, no explanation of jurisdiction, and—crucially—no surviving evidence that this became a durable house custom. The exact phrase does not recur cleanly later in the recovered search. For now it is one Tae-authored morning decree, not a constitutional amendment.

What keeps it from being only an isolated sentence is the same-day callback. Roughly seventy-eight minutes later Tae writes `Gabu`, then `Go to jail`. The gap lets the joke survive long enough to become enforcement: she has not merely shouted a rule and forgotten it in the next message. Gabu is still apparently subject to Tae's temporary government an hour later.

A screenshot sits nearby in the Wall sequence, but the image was not inspected and the handoff does not preserve a safe provenance join. So the wiki does not claim the screenshot caused the sentence, depicts Gabu, or proves any offense. Tae's words are already enough. One morning, she invented a rule against Gabu and then behaved as though she had the authority to administer it.

## Emergency Bible Study, Apparently

On January 26, 2021, `🌈│simp-here` is doing what the room name suggests when Tae abruptly posts `bible study at my house DONT BE LATE HOES`.

The line works because every part of it pulls in a different direction. `bible study` proposes emergency moral reform. `at my house` makes Tae the host. `DONT BE LATE HOES` delivers the invitation in exactly the profanity-heavy register the imaginary intervention is supposed to correct. She does not really step outside the room and scold it. She flips the premise while staying completely inside its social rhythm.

That is useful early voice evidence, but it stays narrow. It says nothing reliable about Tae's religion. What it does show is that by early 2021 she can redirect a ridiculous conversation by becoming equally ridiculous in the opposite direction.

## I Been in Here

On March 13 Ren writes `Tae has become one of us`. Twenty-nine seconds later Tae answers: `I been in here`.

The reply is one of the clearest small character moments in the Platelet shelf because Tae does not reject the belonging. She rejects the idea that it just happened. There is no grateful `finally`, no request for clarification, no performance of being newly welcomed. `I been in here` lands like somebody correcting bad paperwork: the relationship may be affectionate, but the timestamp is wrong.

That distinction matters for chronology. Ren's sentence cannot be used as a membership-start receipt. Tae herself immediately resists that reading. From her side of the conversation, the house is late to announcing something she already considers true.

The same date also preserves Tae using `Caught in 4k` in the Wall. The underlying March exhibit is not safely reconstructed, so the page does not identify a defendant or invent what was caught. The phrase itself is enough to show that Tae already understands one of the room's shortest evidence verdicts.

## Evidence Against Everybody, Including Herself

On May 30 the phrase returns. This time Tae herself posts an attachment to the Wall and, nine seconds later, writes `Caught in 4k`.

The repeat is more informative than either occurrence alone. `Caught in 4k` is not merely a one-off phrase she happened to use in March. By May it is available as a reusable judgment, and Tae can be the person introducing the exhibit as well as the person applying the verdict. The image stays undescribed because attribution discipline matters more than squeezing another joke out of an unseen attachment.

Then October changes the direction of the evidence. In Slice, Tae posts an older photo with the caption `this an old pic but i just look so funny in it:jerrylaugh:`.

That line is not Wall prosecution, but it belongs in the same biography because it shows Tae does not reserve comic evidence for other people. She digs into her own back catalog and makes herself the stated reason to share the image. The wording is casual and specific: not `look how cute I was`, not a nostalgia essay, just `i just look so funny in it` followed by Jerry laughing.

Across those 2021 scenes, the progression is cleaner than a themed list of quirks. January shows Tae inventing a one-day rule, then speaking fluently in room tone. March shows her correcting someone else's idea of when she became `one of us`. March and May show a repeated Wall verdict. October shows a willingness to submit her own image when she finds herself funny enough. The evidence culture is not something merely happening to her; she can operate it and volunteer for it.

## Whatever Rich Is Playing, Bring Rummy

The next strong surfaced scene comes much later, in December 2024, in typed sidecar text around a voice channel. Tae is reacting to a game Rich is playing. She considers installing it, wonders whether Rummy would like it, and says she can join another day if Rich is streaming.

The scene is quieter than the 2021 Wall material, but chronologically it may be more important. Dragon Raja is no longer the thing organizing everybody's attention. Nobody is recruiting Tae into a club objective. Rich is already doing something; Tae's interest moves from watching to possibly installing, then outward to whether Rummy might enjoy it too.

That is what long-term continuity looks like when the original game stops being the center. The object can change because the relationship network is portable. Tae does not need a UL event banner to imagine the next shared activity; a friend playing a game is enough to start the chain.

The spoken call is not preserved, so the site does not pretend to know everything Rich said or showed. The typed half already tells the useful story. In 2021 Tae's `I been in here` rejects the idea that belonging has just been granted. In 2024 the same belonging is mundane enough that it expresses itself as `maybe I install what my friends are playing next`.

Permanent Platelet is the stable role label. Tae's surviving progression is the human part underneath it: one word at the Discord door, an embarrassed algebra question answered seriously, a temporary government whose first known victim is Gabu, fluency in the house's screenshot grammar, self-submitted evidence when the joke is good, and years later friendship rather than one specific game deciding where she shows up.

## Too Much Tae for the Search Box

Tae wondered aloud whether she had left an impression. Mugs was trying to search the old Discord material and said looking up `tae` crashed the search. Tae tried it too and came back with `I DID THE SAME THING AND IT CRASHED MY PC`. Nobody diagnosed either failure. The timing did the comedic work: Tae asked whether anyone would remember her, and two computers apparently refused to answer normally. The August 11 Wall post (`1536935166122008636`) is the source for the exchange, not a technical crash report.

## The Context Stays

On February 24, 2022, Sota filed three Tae screenshots under an actual `Context:` heading, explaining her deliberately ridiculous baby-roleplay voice. Tae answered `wow you added context im honored`. Sota said the footnote was `for your dignity ❤️`; she gave him a disgust reaction. Then he offered to take the context away, and Tae answered `no`.

That last word is the whole episode. Tae was willing to insult the defense attorney, but she was not willing to lose the defense. The Wall could keep its screenshots; apparently it also had to keep the footnotes. The images themselves remain undescribed.

## The Bestie Who Dared the Archivist

Ren called Tae her bestie while introducing a Wall exhibit of Tae's pickup lines. Tae's answer was not damage control: `POST ALL IT YOU WONT`. Ren promised to post something better. Eight seconds was apparently all Tae needed to turn an attempted exposure into a challenge. Neither was arguing over whether the other could keep the material; they were competing over how spectacularly it should be exhibited. The actual lines are still offstage, and the friendship is loud enough without them.
