Beowulf / JereGilgamesh / Jere belongs on the VIP shelf, but the archive never makes him feel like somebody standing stiffly in the foyer waiting for a guest badge. His surviving early-2020 scenes are all movement: compare loot, find the memes, answer the event call, start making the event call yourself, then wave somebody else through the door with `Enjoy our serve` before the record is even six weeks old.

That does not secretly promote him into UL Staff or recruiting command. It does show how quickly the visitor category could stop sounding socially distant.

## April 8, 2020 — loot first, memes immediately after

The first clean scene has Jere saying `My loot is better in beowulf`. In the same Lobby session, Gilli uses the bot to hug Jere, Jere pats Gilli back, and Jere asks where the memes section is.

It is not a grand arrival speech. The sequence is much more useful because it catches practical orientation and social play at the same time. Jere is figuring out where things are while already participating in the room's ordinary affection grammar. The bot command does not establish romance or closeness rank; it simply shows that he is not waiting to become culturally legible before joining in.

The memes question also gives the file a tiny front-door texture: some newcomers ask about rules, some ask about roles, Jere would like directions to the nonsense department.

## April 15 through May 5 — the person answering the call

Across four event announcements, Jere answers with `Alright!!!`, `Roger`, `Omggg`, and `Oooh going now`.

The wording shifts, but the posture does not. He sees the call and moves toward it. `Roger` is especially efficient: no speech about commitment, just acknowledgement that the instruction landed. `Oooh going now` adds the body-language equivalent of already standing up from the chair.

These are small messages, which is why the repetition matters. One RSVP is nothing. A run of them across several weeks establishes a reliable archive rhythm without inventing a formal event role.

## May 12 — the caller changes sides

Then the direction flips.

Jere posts:

`@everyone  star of Cassell now`

Five weeks after the first surfaced Lobby scene, he is no longer only the person answering activity calls. He is making one.

That still does not create a Game Officer or Event Director appointment. The archive gives us behavior, not a promotion ceremony. The useful progression is social: he has learned the room well enough to summon it in the same blunt event grammar he had been answering.

## May 14 — `Enjoy our serve`

Two days later, Jere asks for Shamont to be invited, tags Shamont when they arrive, and later tells another arrival `Enjoy our serve`.

The possessive word is the interesting part: `our`.

On paper, Jere is VIP. In the sentence, he sounds like somebody welcoming a newcomer into a place he already treats as shared. That is exactly why primary-shelf discipline matters. Social ownership and formal membership are not the same thing. The wiki does not need to flatten one into the other to notice the tension.

Mugen performs Shamont's visible role assignment, which keeps the operational boundary clean. Jere can be a bridge without being rewritten as the recruiter who held the key.

## May 20 — one sentence acquires media attachments

A week later Jere says `We are so far away from 95`.

The archive does not safely tell us what `95` means. Instead of filling the blank with vibes, the file watches what Jere does next: repeats variants of `so far away`, writes `Maybe dragon force can help me to say`, drops a `How Far?` / Waze-style Tenor GIF, and then posts a YouTube link.

The joke expands sideways across formats. Text is apparently insufficient, so he recruits a GIF and then a song-shaped object to carry the same complaint.

That is the safe character evidence. We do not know the target. We do know that Jere can take one vague frustration and keep decorating it until the room has received the multimedia edition.

## The arc the archive actually earns

The file is short in calendar time but not empty in progression. April catches Jere orienting himself while already social. The following weeks repeatedly catch him answering activity calls. In May he starts issuing one, then helps route another person through the front door, then uses `our` while welcoming someone else.

VIP remains the correct role label. The behavior simply shows how quickly the category could become socially porous in UL. Beowulf does not need an invented title to feel like part of the house. The receipts already show the transition from finding the memes room to acting like the server is somewhere he can help other people enter.
