Zoshaa has one of the clearest early-UL progressions in the Platelet shelf because the archive catches both directions of the relationship. At first the house is teaching Zoshaa where things go and rapidly turning NintendoShitcube into one of its reusable family nouns. A year later Zoshaa is no longer only the person being named, routed, or joked about. She is explicitly making local meme edits and putting new objects back into circulation herself.

The identity bridge matters throughout: `NintendoShitcube` and `nintendoshitcube_` are Zoshaa, not a second Platelet who wandered in from a cursed Nintendo subsidiary.

## 2020 — arriving before the house itself knows what it is doing

On April 25, 2020, Zoshaa introduces themself in the Lobby and says `I also have no idea wot I’m doing`.

Less than three weeks later the archive gives that disclaimer a tiny sequel. In Slice, Mugen tells NintendoShitcube `memes go under meme wars bb 😄`.

The correction is gentle enough to show the scale of the incident. There is no disciplinary crisis here: `bb`, smiley, correct door. But chronologically it is perfect onboarding evidence. Zoshaa arrives with zero procedural confidence and almost immediately needs one piece of municipal guidance from Mugen about which room handles memes. Early UL is still a house where people are learning the floor plan in public.

By June the social language around Zoshaa is already becoming more specific. Gilli directly addresses NintendoShitcube as `C H I L D`. By September that label has acquired fake maintenance obligations: `We havent given the child tide pods in a while`, then `We so neglectful`, followed by the NintendoShitcube tag and a Tide-Pods Tenor.

The joke is intentionally grotesque and must stay a joke. Nobody is documenting detergent consumption or literal neglect. What the sequence actually proves is that `the child` has become stable enough as a house noun around Zoshaa that Gilli can build an absurd care-duty premise on top of it without stopping to define who she means.

Then September 29 moves the same social labeling into UL's own fake-family infrastructure. Anthos's pinned Tumbleweed / `Underlords Organisation Update` includes `RV - Zoshaa (orb) - child shield`.

That line is not biological kinship, guardianship, ownership, or a formal job called Child Shield. It is relationship-map syntax. But its placement is still meaningful: in less than six months, the newcomer who said `I have no idea wot I’m doing` has become legible enough to the group that the house is filing her under its own stupid nouns.

## 2021 — the nouns persist, while Zoshaa starts adding objects of her own

April 24, 2021 gives the biography a useful turn. In Meems, Zoshaa posts an image with the sentence `Made another one`.

Here the attribution is unusually clean. The recovered attachment was visually inspected as a Ring/front-door-style image locally edited with a blurred running black cat, and Zoshaa's own maker wording sits on the same message. That supports **made by Zoshaa** for the local edit. It does not magically transfer ownership of the underlying source photograph or every element in the template.

The word `another` is small but helpful. Zoshaa talks as though making this kind of object is already familiar in the immediate conversational world. It does not let the archive identify the first-ever edit, but it does show that the person who needed `memes go under meme wars bb` in May 2020 is now contributing a made object to the media stream herself.

A few weeks later, on May 17, the Wall catches the authored line `I’d share a refreshing glass of boiled mayo with my orb any day ᵘ ʷ ᵘ`.

The delivery is very Zoshaa in the surviving record: an objectively disgusting image presented with soft decorative text and no hesitation. The page does not resolve `my orb` to the Tumbleweed's directional `Zoshaa (orb)` edge; the referent is not earned. The line still helps establish voice — compact, weirdly specific, and completely committed to the sentence it has chosen to inflict on everybody.

By August 5 the 2020 house nouns are still alive. Anayss tags NintendoShitcube with `missing the c h i l d ~`; Baby Lyssa says `the child`; Ren says `The o r b`; Zoshaa later contributes `Screaming is what I do best😎😎😎`; Snow eventually writes `~mutes the child~`.

The opening attachment remains uninspected and Snow's `mute` is banter, not a proven moderation action. The important longitudinal point is simpler: `child` and `orb` did not vanish after the 2020 Tumbleweed session. A year later multiple people can use them around Zoshaa without reintroducing the bit. The metadata has become portable.

## 2022 — making remains visible

On January 11, 2022, Zoshaa posts another clean maker receipt: `I made a thing`, attached to a two-PNG edit sequence. The visually inspected altered version replaces the original large creature with a long-neck bug and changes the caption.

Again, the language is almost aggressively modest. No portfolio announcement, no formal Media title, no declaration that Zoshaa is UL's editor. Just `I made a thing` and the thing. That modest wording is exactly why the receipt is so useful: it joins author statement and local object without requiring the site to infer creative credit from proximity.

The following day NintendoShitcube comments in the Wall: `What a stupid looking animal, glad I’m not a bug with a long neck`.

Taken next to the Meems edit, the line keeps the same short-form comic instinct visible. The archive does not need to dub Zoshaa `random` or invent an artist persona. It can say something more precise. Her surviving contribution style is often compact: a weird object, a short maker sentence, a specific animal insult, then the conversation moves on.

Across 2020 to 2022, that gives Zoshaa a real progression instead of a drawer full of themed bits. She arrives unsure of the room layout. The house quickly names her `child` and `orb`, then writes those labels into the Tumbleweed. A year later those nouns still work socially — but Zoshaa is also visibly participating in the opposite direction, manufacturing local edits that other people can now react to.

Permanent Platelet is the roster continuity. The richer story is that UL spends the early archive turning Zoshaa into a reusable piece of house language, while Zoshaa gradually becomes one of the people making new pieces of the house's visual language in return.
