# UL Evidence Standard — Lower Threshold, Hard Locks Stay Hard

This rule applies to archive mining, character biographies, integration, wiki synthesis, relationship writing, incident reconstruction, and lore/motif work.

## Contextual confidence is enough

Hard user-confirmed canon locks, resolved identity corrections, privacy/safety boundaries, explicit source contradictions, and high-risk attribution boundaries remain hard.

Outside those boundaries, **do not require courtroom-grade proof for ordinary human interpretation**.

Strong contextual inference is usable when the surrounding archive makes a read more likely than not. This includes probable relationship texture, tastes, habits, dislikes, ordinary-life details, work/school texture, humor mechanisms, repeated behavior, likely callbacks/origin hypotheses, peer descriptions, cumulative character reads, and plausible chronology/background.

A finding labeled **inferred**, **probable**, or **likely** is not second-class. Miners should surface it rather than quarantining it merely because no single message explicitly states the conclusion. Integrators and wiki editors should not reject it solely for lacking a declarative receipt.

Use natural confidence language (`apparently`, `likely`, `seems to have`, etc.) only when uncertainty materially matters. Do not bury obvious human reads under caveat sludge.

## What remains strict

Stay strict about hard canon locks and corrections, identity merges/splits, sensitive/private facts, formal appointment chronology when conflicting, joke roles/relationships being literalized, SAID BY / POSTED BY / MADE BY / CAPTURED BY / FEATURING attribution, and genuinely contradictory evidence.

`Earliest surviving` still does not automatically equal `origin`; however, a strong likely-origin or callback hypothesis may be presented as such instead of being exiled forever.

The goal is an archive-first fandom history populated by recognizable people, not an evidentiary tax court.
